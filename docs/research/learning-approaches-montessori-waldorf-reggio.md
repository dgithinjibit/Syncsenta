# Learning-approach research: Montessori, Waldorf, Reggio Emilia

Research performed in the connected browser on 28 September 2026. It extends
[`omega-claw-learning-methods.md`](omega-claw-learning-methods.md), which covers
the four approaches already in the system: Suzuki (environment, listening and
imitation), Kumon (diagnosis, small steps, mastery gate), UDL (access and
expression options) and PBLWorks project-based inquiry (an authentic community
question).

The point of this pass is not to collect quotes. It is to answer, for each new
approach, three questions this repo has to be able to act on: **what decision
does it change in the product**, **what does it forbid or make awkward**, and
**what is the primary source that says so**.

---

## Sources and findings

### American Montessori Society / AMI USA — Montessori philosophy

Source: https://www.amiusa.org/montessori-philosophy

The approach is described as "a child-centered approach to education that
emphasizes independence, freedom within limits, and respect for a child's
natural development". The elements that matter for us: a **prepared
environment** of "materials arranged to encourage exploration and order" so the
room itself produces "independence and self-directed learning"; the **absorbent
mind**, a "unique ability to absorb knowledge from their surroundings
effortlessly"; **sensitive periods**, times when "children are especially
receptive to acquiring certain skills"; and **freedom within limits** — "freedom
to choose their activities within a structured environment, which supports both
autonomy and discipline". Education is framed as "an aid to life", for the "full
development of the human being".

### Montessori Society (UK) — principles and planes

Source: https://www.montessorisociety.org.uk/principles

The adult's role is stated as guides who "act as a guide to help the child find
his natural path of development", and the environment must be "an environment
that serves the particular needs of the child's stage of development". The
learner gets "freedom for the child to engage in his own development according
to his own particular timeline" — i.e. the pacing is the child's, not the
cohort's.

### Montessori Australia — the four planes of development

Source: https://my.montessori.org.au/montessori-planes-of-development/

Montessori describes development as "a series of rebirths" in four planes, and
each plane implies a different mode of teaching:

| Plane | Ages | Characteristic | What the learner needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | birth–6 | the "Absorbent Mind" | a ordered environment; "Help me to do it by myself." |
| II | 6–12 | the "Reasoning Mind" | to classify and to see how things connect — Cosmic Education and the Great Lessons |
| III | 12–18 | the "Social Self" | a "social birth": purpose, real work, independence (Erdkinder — self-built work at a farm/community) |
| IV | 18–24 | maturity | specialised work in the world |

The grade bands in this table map onto Kenyan schooling almost for free, by age:
Plane I (to 6) is PP1–PP2; Plane II (6–12) is Lower and Upper Primary, Grade 1–6,
which is exactly where CBC's subject strands multiply; Plane III (12–18) is Junior
and Senior School, Grade 7–12. That is a useful external check on the grade-range
work already open in the curriculum tasks: the *mode* of teaching has to change at
those boundaries, not just the content.

### Waldorf School of Palm Beach — the curriculum, decoded

Source: https://www.waldorfschoolpalmbeach.org/blog-entries/waldorf-curriculum

What is taught, and when, is developmental rather than grade-driven. Early
childhood (0–7) is "learning primarily through imitation… and through
unstructured, imaginative play", and formal academics are deliberately delayed.
Literacy begins in grade 1 through "letters as living forms"; grades 1–3 run on
storytelling, fables and practical work; grades 4–6 widen into "Norse mythology
and fractions" in grade 4, then ancient history and physics; grades 7–8 take on
"algebra, plane geometry, chemistry, and original research writing". Structure:
daily "main lesson blocks" (one subject dwelled with for weeks), a class teacher
who stays "with the same group of students for multiple years, often grades 1
through 5", and creative disciplines "woven directly into every academic
subject". Assessment is not a score: the student's own main-lesson book is
"their own record of what they have learned, in their own hand".

### International Waldorf School (NL) — basic principles

Source: https://www.internationalwaldorfschool.nl/our-school/basic-principles/

Waldorf education works the whole child — "Head (cognition), Heart (social &
emotional endowments) and Hands (physical skills and perseverance)" — and
challenges pupils on "intellectual, creative, artistic and social levels".
Development is read in broad phases: early childhood "concentrated around the
physical body", then the emotional/somatic phase, then analytical thinking
arriving later. Rhythm is built into the year ("throughout the year, we
celebrate several festivals"), and assessment is observational: "Teachers
determine the developmental phase of each child by observation."

### Bright Water Waldorf School — media policy

Source: https://www.brightwaterwaldorf.org/media-policy

This is the sharpest conflict with what SyncSenta is. For birth–7 parents commit
"not to expose their children to electronic media and screens during their
early childhood years", because "exposure to media images does not support the
imagination of the growing child". For 7–11: "no media exposure on school nights
and mornings", "no smartphone usage", and before age ten "serious restriction or
no exposure" to media images, because "the importance of a free imaginative life
is paramount". For 12–14: they are "too young to navigate the dangerous world of
social media and this is better left for high school", and "no middle school
child [is requested] to use smartphones" except for homework help.

### Reggio Children — the hundred languages

Source: https://www.reggiochildren.it/en/100-languages-month/

The official institute states the approach rests on "the image of a child with
strong potentialities for development and a subject with rights". Children learn
and express through "the hundred languages belonging to all human beings", which
Malaguzzi described as "ways of being and acting, generators of complex imagery
and vocabularies". The institutional obligation is to value "the expressivity of
all languages, verbal and non-verbal, with equal dignity" — i.e.
a drawing, a model, a recording and a written answer are *equal* evidence of the
same understanding.

### Early Excellence — the Reggio approach in practice

Source: https://earlyexcellence.com/practice-and-pedagogy/reggio-emilia-approach/

Summarises the working parts: "strong respect for the child's role in his or her
own learning process"; children "have a hundred languages"; the physical setting
is pedagogical, since the "learning environment is a powerful teacher";
"meaningful community collaboration" with families who should "feel well" in the
space; and practice made visible, so that schools become "documentable".
Documentation is the discipline that makes the other four inspectable: the
teacher records what the child said, made, and revised, and reads it back.

### KICD — Basic Education Curriculum Framework (compatibility anchor, not yet read in this pass)

Source: https://kicd.ac.ke/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/CURRICULUMFRAMEWORK.pdf

Cited here as the document that governs whether any of the above may enter the
product at all. **Not read during this session** — the claims below about CBC
compatibility are marked as unverified until that PDF (and the senior-school
documents already in flight) are checked against it.

## What each approach actually changes in SyncSenta

The four existing approaches answer *how a learner progresses* (Kumon), *how a
learner is first welcomed into a subject* (Suzuki), *how a learner can access
and express* (UDL), and *what the learning is for* (PBL). The three new ones
answer three different questions, which is why they are worth adding rather than
padding:

| Approach | The decision it changes | Where that lands in the code |
|---|---|---|
| **Montessori** | *Who chooses the next activity, and on whose clock.* A prepared environment offers a bounded set of choices; the learner picks; the guide observes instead of assigning. | The learner-facing surface: the student home / subject picker (`app/student`, `components/student/competency-map.tsx`), and `omega-claw-rules.ts` — where "the engine decides the next step" is exactly what a Montessori reading would soften into "the engine offers 2–3 prepared options and records which was chosen". |
| **Waldorf** | *When a subject is introduced, and in what mode.* Imagination and story before abstraction; one subject dwelled with in a block rather than eight topics a day; the same teacher staying with a class; the learner's own book as the record. | Curriculum sequencing (`lib/curriculum/cbc-curriculum.ts`, the grade waterfall) and the schedule shape: a "main lesson" slot in the daily plan, and `teacher_students` / class-teacher looping in the assignment tables. |
| **Reggio Emilia** | *What counts as evidence, and what the teacher keeps.* Multiple equal languages of expression, plus systematic pedagogical documentation of the process, plus parents as co-participants. | `learning_evidence`, `sandbox_artifacts`, `chat_messages` and the parent surfaces — documentation is a *first-class artefact type*, not a side-effect of telemetry. |

## The three frictions, stated before anyone codes

These are the reasons a "just add them" implementation would be dishonest, and
each needs a decision rather than a workaround.

1. **Waldorf is explicitly anti-screen at precisely the ages we serve.** The
   published position is no media before roughly age 7 and serious restriction
   to age 10, on the grounds that media images displace imagination. SyncSenta is
   a web app used by PP1–Grade 9. Adopting Waldorf therefore means either
   (a) adopting only its developmental staging, rhythm, block structure and
   arts integration and *saying so in the boundary line*, (b) adding real
   offline/no-screen activity prompts and parent guidance so the approach
   changes behaviour rather than marketing, or (c) not calling it Waldorf.
   (a) + (b) is the defensible combination; (c) is the honest alternative if we
   will not do (b).
2. **Montessori and Reggio both reject extrinsic reward; our system has points,
   badges and streaks.** `profiles.total_points`, the `achievements` table, and
   `daily_activity.daily_streak` are gamification, which Montessori practice
   treats as a substitute for intrinsic satisfaction and Reggio does not
   recognise at all. This does not require deleting gamification — it requires a
   decision about which surface leads for a young learner: mastery language
   ("you can now do X") or points language ("you earned 20 points").
3. **Reggio documentation is interpretive and teacher-made; our `omega_scaffolding_events`
   is automatic machine telemetry.** They look similar (both are append-only rows
   per episode) and they are not the same thing. Documentation needs the child's
   own words/artefact plus a teacher's reading of it. If we want Reggio to be
   real, the `learning_evidence` table plus `sandbox_artifacts` need a
   teacher-authored annotation and a view a parent can read — which is also
   exactly what CBC's portfolio/assessment expectations want, once verified
   against the KICD framework.

## Implementation decision (confirmed with the account holder, 2026-09-28)

**Model: blend all five, one registry.** There is no per-school or per-class
pedagogy profile and no schema change; the memory layer went into production the
same day and every new column should be justified by data from real usage first.
The five approaches are applied together, the way the existing four already are.
If a profile is wanted later it is one nullable column plus a lookup, not a
rewrite, so nothing here forecloses it.

As with the four existing approaches, these are **adaptations of principles**,
not claims that SyncSenta is a Montessori, Waldorf or Reggio Emilia programme,
and each entry must carry a `boundary` line saying what it is *not* — the same
discipline `studio/src/curriculum/omega-claw-ai-blockchain.ts` already applies to
Suzuki ("do not turn Omega Claw into music instruction or claim Suzuki
certification") and Kumon ("do not use worksheet volume, competition, or
acceleration as the goal").

**Friction 1 — Waldorf's screen position: hybrid.** Adopt the developmental
sequence (story and image before abstraction, one subject dwelled with, arts
inside the academic task) *and* take the age limit seriously rather than
ignoring it: the youngest plane gets a capped session posture and must be routed
to an offline activity, and the registry states plainly that SyncSenta is not a
Waldorf school and does not reproduce its media policy. What we explicitly do
not do is advertise Waldorf while giving a six-year-old an open-ended screen.

**Friction 2 — extrinsic reward: hybrid, age-gated by plane.** For Montessori
Plane I–II (PP1–Grade 6, ages 4–12) the learner-facing surface is mastery
language ("you can now do X"); `profiles.total_points`, `achievements` and
`daily_activity.daily_streak` keep recording and stay visible in the teacher and
parent views. From Plane III (Grade 7 upward, ages 12+) points, streaks and badges
may lead, which is closer to each approach's own age theory than a single rule
applied to a five-year-old and a seventeen-year-old.

**Friction 3 — documentation vs telemetry: kept separate.** `omega_scaffolding_events`
stays automatic machine telemetry. Reggio-style documentation is adult-authored,
interpretive, and attaches to a learner artefact, so it belongs to
`learning_evidence` and `sandbox_artifacts` with a teacher annotation and a
parent-readable view — which is also what CBC's portfolio and assessment
expectations want, once verified against the KICD framework.

Concretely, the change set is:

1. ✅ This research file in `docs/research/`.
2. ✅ The prose lists in `docs/curriculum/OMEGA_CLAW_CURRICULUM.md` and
   `docs/curriculum/AI_BLOCKCHAIN_CURRICULUM_ROADMAP.md`, which both said
   "Suzuki-inspired… Kumon-inspired…" and would have become wrong if left alone.
3. ⏳ A **course-neutral pedagogy registry**. Today the only machine-readable
   pedagogy lives in the `adaptations[]` + `sources[]` arrays of
   `studio/src/curriculum/omega-claw-ai-blockchain.ts` — a file named for one
   course. Seven approaches cannot live there; the registry moves to its own
   module and the AI/blockchain course reads it. (Same "one source of truth"
   problem already tracked for the role field.)
4. ⏳ The tutor prompt: `studio/src/lib/mwalimu-pipeline.ts:291` emits
   `# PEDAGOGY CONSTRAINT (from MeTTa Suzuki/Kumon rules)` — a hardcoded label
   that will lie the moment a third approach shapes a reply. It must be generated
   from the registry, boundary lines included, or the approaches are decoration.
5. ⏳ The age posture the two hybrids depend on: one pure, tested function mapping
   grade → Montessori plane → `{sessionCap, offlineActivityRequired,
   learnerFacingRewardSurface}`. That single function is what makes Frictions 1
   and 2 real instead of aspirational.
6. ⏳ The MeTTa rule pack — only for what is mechanically checkable (e.g. "offer a
   bounded choice and record which was taken"; "one subject dwelled with per
   block"). Note the constraint in `omega-claw-rules.test.ts`: the TypeScript
   mirror and `backend/syncsenta-backend/data/omega_claw_rules.metta` are parsed
   against each other and the suite fails when they disagree, so any new rule has
   to be written twice, in the same change, and the Rust file is the one that will
   outlive us.

Explicitly **not** in scope: any schema change. No `pedagogy` column on
`profiles` or `school_classes`, no new table. The memory layer went into
production on 2026-09-28; a column that says which approach a class follows is
only worth adding once there is usage data saying the blend is not enough.
